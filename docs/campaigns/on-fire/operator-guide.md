# ON FIRE — operator guide

Prepared 25 October 2026. Owner: Adrian. Issue: [#746](https://github.com/adrianwedd/adrianwedd.com/issues/746). **Do not launch without Adrian's explicit approval.** This is a manual email workflow, separate from the paid diagnostic and the automated ops enquiry pipeline. Nothing here sends mail, scans websites or changes production.

## One inbox, one private record

Applications arrive in the existing Google-hosted mailbox at `adrian@adrianwedd.com` address with subject `ON FIRE — free website exposure review`. The public link opens an editable email draft; it does not submit an application or guarantee delivery. The page also exposes the full text and address without JavaScript or an email app.

The canonical award record is `~/.local/share/on-fire-campaign/ledger.json` on Adrian's machine. Use `scripts/on-fire/ledger.py` to maintain it. It has a process lock, atomic writes, private directory/file permissions and a lifetime limit of ten awards. No new service, database, credentials or recurring spend. Do not keep a second ledger, put client data in GitHub, or operate copied ledgers on two hosts. Other machines must use the canonical host; OS file locks do not coordinate replicas. Back up the private directory using existing protected backups, not this public repository.

Generic workflow and templates are versioned here, outside `src/` and `public/`; Astro does not publish them as website pages. **Client reports, applications, filled templates and evidence must stay outside the repository**, under the private campaign directory. No secrets belong in the application or ledger. Shell history must contain commands and opaque record IDs, not client data.

## From enquiry to delivery

1. Read the mailbox in received order. Record the actual timezone-qualified receipt timestamp, not when you happened to open the message. If a thread is already present, keep the earliest complete enquiry and merge later clarifications. Acknowledge receipt manually using the template below. Do not click unfamiliar links before reviewing the hostname; never open submitted attachments by default.
2. Confirm business name, public website, contact name/email, location in Huon Valley or southern Tasmania, and explicit written authority to represent that website. Sole traders, family businesses and small not-for-profits qualify. Resolve marginal geography sensibly and record the decision privately. If required details or authority are missing, ask for them; no valid application or award exists yet. Preserve the original enquiry timestamp when clarified. Do not investigate until authority is confirmed.
3. Create a private intake JSON from the email using a local editor (never put it under this checkout). Exact allowed keys: `business`, `website`, `location`, `contact`, `email`, `authorised` (boolean true, only after written confirmation), `received_at` (mailbox ISO timestamp with timezone), `reference` (private message ID, not message body), optional `business_key`. No concern text is stored in the ledger; it stays in email. The public site URL cannot contain credentials, query strings or fragments.
4. Run `python3 scripts/on-fire/ledger.py receive < ~/.local/share/on-fire-campaign/intake.json`. It returns an opaque ID, for example `F001`. Remove the temporary private intake after recording it. A duplicate canonical hostname, including a `www` variant, returns the existing record. For a business with multiple domains, explicitly use its original stable `business_key` (default is its first hostname). Review business identities manually: a host alone cannot identify every business. Distinct businesses on the same shared platform require distinct verified business keys. Email spam and malformed/unauthorised requests are filtered administratively and consume no place. The tool validates fields again; it never fetches a submitted URL.
5. Mark eligibility: `python3 scripts/on-fire/ledger.py eligible F001`. Ineligible or unwanted enquiries: `declined F001`; withdrawal: `withdrawn F001`. Record the reason in private correspondence, not public issue comments. Declined enquiries consume no place.
6. Check `python3 scripts/on-fire/ledger.py status`. Review all earlier mailbox enquiries before awarding, including incomplete ones not yet admitted to the ledger. Resolve their eligibility/authority first; the ledger cannot know about messages you have not imported. Unresolved earlier submissions must be clarified, declined or withdrawn before a later award. Then run `python3 scripts/on-fire/ledger.py award F001`. The ledger refuses an eleventh award and repeated awards do not consume extra places. Awarded, in-progress, delivered and post-award withdrawn records all keep consuming the original place. Do not promise an award until this command succeeds. The lock is held through read, count, order check and atomic replacement. This is local administration, not a public application endpoint.
7. Send the award email personally. Agree on a realistic delivery date in that email before beginning; do not advertise a turnaround you cannot meet. Mark `in-progress F001`. Copy `report-template.md` into `~/.local/share/on-fire-campaign/reports/F001.md` and fill it privately.
8. Perform only the passive, bounded review below. Keep timestamped source references and minimal evidence in the private report directory. Never paste customer details or findings into the public campaign issue.
9. Read the report as a business owner: what do we know, what don't we know, what should happen next? Send a one- or two-page report personally to the verified contact, with the delivery template. Read back recipient/address and attachment before sending. Do not CC other businesses. Mark `delivered F001` only after delivery; preserve the sent-message reference privately. Confirm that the owner understands the action or invite a reply.
10. On the tenth award, `status` returns `closed: true` with `closed_at`. If the offer must end earlier, run `python3 scripts/on-fire/ledger.py close`, then check `status`. Closing is permanent: it blocks new intake and new awards, preserves all lifetime awards and starts the 90-day retention clock at the first closure. Repeating `close` preserves that date. Existing awards can still move to in-progress, delivered or withdrawn without reopening intake or recycling places. Change `src/data/on-fire.json` to `"closed": true` and publish through the normal authorised release route promptly, before sending more invitations. The page removes the email application CTA and changes title, description and OG image while retaining all advice. `/local/` uses the same state file and changes its link text automatically. This is a static site: the public CTA cannot change until that deployment succeeds. While a closure update is pending, reply honestly to incoming mail; no email action ever promises availability. Do not publish a numerical counter. Check the rendered page and social metadata after release and refresh cached previews through platform tools where available. Keep the stable URL.

## Passive assessment scope

The written application permits an initial external, non-invasive review of the submitted website only. Check public DNS, certificate validity/hostname, ordinary public response headers, explicitly disclosed technology information, authoritative advisories and obvious public configuration. Ordinary page requests should be few and normal; don't crawl, scan or probe for hidden resources. Record any redirects and stop if the target belongs to a different business until scope is clarified. Do not execute or automatically fetch URLs supplied in intake; review them first. Avoid authenticated cookies and don't retain page content containing personal data.

Treat response headers and fingerprints as hints. A public `PHP/x.y` header does not prove the runtime used throughout a site, and missing fingerprints mean unknown. A patched version does not establish historical absence of compromise. Vendor backports require vendor evidence. Obtain logs, hosting/runtime details, patch dates and restoration evidence through the authorised owner/provider; don't request credentials. Review the latest primary advisories at assessment time, including PHP's 23 October session warning. Don't copy mitigation configuration into production: advise the responsible host to apply and verify appropriate changes.

For every material finding use one classification: **confirmed**, **probable with supporting evidence**, **possible but unverified**, **not assessable externally**, or **not applicable**. Explain evidence, limits and what would resolve uncertainty. Prioritise likely business impact, urgency and dependencies; avoid traffic-light certification or unsupported CVSS-derived certainty.

No exploit payloads, credential attacks, endpoint fuzzing, forced browsing, authenticated testing, mass port scans, production stress tests or denial-of-service probes. Those require a separate written scope, actual system ownership and compliance with hosting-provider conditions. An application is not that permission. Never investigate neighbouring shared-hosting tenants, unrelated businesses or random local websites.

If credible active compromise is suspected, stop routine review. Privately alert the verified owner with the evidence and uncertainty. Recommend urgent contact with the host and a qualified incident responder; preserve logs/evidence, agree containment with the system owner and use a known-clean device for sensitive administration. Exposed credentials should be revoked/rotated through the provider's response process, with service dependencies considered. Do not delete evidence, shut down a business service or promise forensic investigation/remediation as part of this offer. The provider/incident responder should own detailed containment and any notification obligations.

## Privacy, retention and operational limits

Only Adrian administers the local record; email/storage infrastructure providers process their respective copies. Use existing device security and protected backups. No marketing list, CRM, public participant list, new tracker, AI analysis of submissions or autonomous customer communication. The manual inbox relies on the existing mail provider's spam/abuse controls; there is no new public POST endpoint or claim of web-form rate limiting. Repeated email creates no additional award. Do not forward intake into the automated ops sales system.

Set a private calendar reminder for **90 days after closure**. Delete campaign email, reports, intake files and evidence then, unless the person requests continued retention or a legal requirement applies. `python3 scripts/on-fire/ledger.py purge` refuses before 90 days and then removes contact data, website hashes and correspondence references, retaining anonymous place-consumption tombstones. Backups expire on their provider schedules; do not promise immediate erasure from backups. Handle an earlier individual deletion request manually: remove identifying fields/evidence from that person's private record while preserving `id`, `status`, `awarded_at` and enough non-identifying queue history. Do not remove an awarded record or reset the count. Check private backups and mailbox copies under the existing retention process.

## Launch and closure controls

The campaign is held on its PR. `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` builds PRs without deploying them; push to `main` triggers build and the production Pages deployment. **Merging this PR launches the campaign.** Do not merge, enable auto-merge, dispatch Pages, change secrets or deploy a Worker during preparation.

Before requesting launch approval: check current advisories and date labels; replace “Publication date will be recorded…” with the actual approved publication date; verify independent external-mailbox receipt/reply and spam placement under the authorised test scope; confirm the private ledger location and report template; read the page in dark/light mobile and desktop; ensure required PR checks are green at the exact head. Then Adrian's explicit launch approval permits the normal merge. Read back the successful Pages run and live `/on-fire/`, `/local/`, privacy section, canonical and both social preview tags. A source build isn't evidence of production.

At early closure, run `python3 scripts/on-fire/ledger.py close`; at capacity, closure is recorded automatically. Check `status` for the original `closed_at`, and set the private retention reminder for 90 days after that date. Update the shared campaign state and actual review date after reviewing; verify the closed build and its OG image. Never reset the ledger, recycle withdrawn/delivered places or add an “only N left” claim. Rebuilding the page has no authority to award a place.

Incremental recurring cost: **A$0 new services**; existing email, static hosting and device backups remain in use. No subscriptions, cloud databases, scheduled AI work or new paid CI. Human review, email administration, closure publication and retention cleanup are manual obligations.
